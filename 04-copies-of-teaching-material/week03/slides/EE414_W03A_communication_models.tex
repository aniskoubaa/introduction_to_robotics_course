\section{Three shapes of interaction}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}{The question to ask before you write anything}
  \begin{beamercolorbox}[sep=8pt,center,rounded=true]{palette secondary}
    \small ``Is this a \textbf{stream}, a \textbf{question}, or a \textbf{job}?''
  \end{beamercolorbox}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=0.35cm and 0.7cm, font=\scriptsize]
    \node[sense, minimum width=3.1cm, minimum height=1.2cm, align=center] (t)
      {\textbf{Stream}\\ never ends\\ nobody waits};
    \node[plan, minimum width=3.1cm, minimum height=1.2cm, align=center, right=of t] (s)
      {\textbf{Question}\\ answered at once\\ caller waits};
    \node[act, minimum width=3.1cm, minimum height=1.2cm, align=center, right=of s] (a)
      {\textbf{Job}\\ takes time\\ may be cancelled};
    \node[below=0.15cm of t, font=\scriptsize\ttfamily] {topic};
    \node[below=0.15cm of s, font=\scriptsize\ttfamily] {service};
    \node[below=0.15cm of a, font=\scriptsize\ttfamily] {action};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.8em}
  \small
  Getting this wrong does not produce an error message. It produces a robot that works in the
  lab and hangs on demo day. The three failure modes are on the next four slides.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Deciding, in practice}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.9cm}p{1.7cm}p{4.9cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{What you are doing} & \textbf{Shape} & \textbf{Why} \\
    \midrule
    Publishing LiDAR scans & topic & Continuous. Nobody waits for a scan. \\[0.2em]
    Sending wheel velocities & topic & Continuous. The next one supersedes it. \\[0.2em]
    ``Reset the odometry to zero'' & service & One question, answered immediately \\[0.2em]
    ``Is the battery healthy?'' & service & A value you need \emph{now}, on demand \\[0.2em]
    ``Drive to the kitchen'' & action & Forty seconds. You want progress. You may cancel. \\[0.2em]
    ``Follow this trajectory'' & action & Same: long, cancellable, worth reporting on \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \begin{underhood}[The two-second test]
    \footnotesize
    If the caller would be unhappy blocking until it finishes, it is not a service. If nobody
    is waiting for an answer at all, it is a topic.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Failure 1 --- a topic where you needed an answer}
  \small
  You publish ``reset the odometry'' on a topic and carry on.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    Did anyone receive it? & You do not know. \\
    Did it succeed? & You do not know. \\
    Was anyone even listening? & You do not know. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.8em}
  A topic is \textbf{fire and forget}. That is a feature for a scan arriving ten times a second
  --- the next one repairs any loss. It is a defect for a command that happens once.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{inthefield}
    \footnotesize
    This is why \texttt{/cmd\_vel} is still a topic and ``reset odometry'' is not. A dropped
    velocity is corrected 50\,ms later. A dropped reset is silently never done.
  \end{inthefield}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Failure 2 --- a service for something slow}
  \small
  ``Navigate to the kitchen'' as a service. It is one request and one reply, so it fits.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=1.6cm, font=\scriptsize]
    \node[plan, minimum width=2.6cm] (c) {your node};
    \node[act,  minimum width=2.6cm, right=of c] (s) {navigator};
    \draw[dataflow] (c) -- node[above,font=\tiny] {``go to kitchen''} (s);
    \draw[dataflow] (s.south) to[out=-90,in=-90] node[below,font=\tiny] {40 s later: ``done''} (c.south);
    \node[below=1.3cm of c, font=\tiny, text=danger, align=center] {blocked\\ for 40 seconds};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  For forty seconds your node answers nothing, reports nothing, and cannot be told to stop.
  If an obstacle appears, or the user changes their mind, there is no mechanism to say so.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \textbf{No feedback. No cancellation. No visibility.} That is what actions exist to fix.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Failure 3 --- the deadlock you will write at least once}
  \small
  Calling a service \textbf{synchronously, from inside a callback}, is the classic ROS 2 hang:

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.2cm}p{5.8cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{What you wrote} & \textbf{What happens} \\
    \midrule
    A subscriber callback that calls a service and waits for the reply
      & The callback blocks. \texttt{spin} is inside that callback, so it cannot process
        the reply. The reply never arrives. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.7em}
  \begin{underhood}[Why]
    \footnotesize
    \texttt{spin} is single-threaded by default. It runs \textbf{one} callback at a time. A
    callback that waits for something \texttt{spin} must deliver is waiting for itself.
  \end{underhood}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \footnotesize
  The fix is \texttt{call\_async} and a future. You will cause this on purpose in session B ---
  it is much easier to remember once you have watched your own node hang.
\end{frame}

% ===============================================================
\section{Writing the contract}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}[fragile]{A message definition, field by field}
  \small A \texttt{.msg} file is a list of \emph{type name} pairs. That is all it is.

  \vspace{0.3em}
  {\footnotesize\texttt{ee414\_w03\_interfaces/msg/WheelState.msg}}
  \begin{spec}
std_msgs/Header  header        <- stamp + frame_id
float64          left_rad_s    <- left wheel angular velocity
float64          right_rad_s   <- right wheel angular velocity
bool             estopped
  \end{spec}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{center}\footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    Primitives & \texttt{bool int8 int32 int64 float32 float64 string} \\
    Arrays & \texttt{float64[]} unbounded, \texttt{float64[16]} fixed, \texttt{float64[<=8]} bounded \\
    Nested & any other message type, e.g.\ \texttt{geometry\_msgs/Pose} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \footnotesize These compile into Python and C++ classes. A misspelt field is a build error.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{When to define a custom message --- and when not to}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \column{0.47\textwidth}
    \begin{block}{\small Define one when}
      \footnotesize
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The data genuinely has no standard type
        \item Fields must travel together, atomically, with one timestamp
        \item It is your own robot's internal state
      \end{itemize}
    \end{block}

    \column{0.47\textwidth}
    \begin{block}{\small Do \emph{not} when}
      \footnotesize
      \begin{itemize}
        \item A standard type already fits --- use it
        \item You only want to rename fields
        \item You are avoiding reading the standard definition
      \end{itemize}
    \end{block}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{inthefield}
    \footnotesize
    \textbf{Every custom type is a wall.} Publish your own \texttt{MyLaserScan} and RViz2 cannot
    display it, \texttt{slam\_toolbox} cannot consume it, and nothing in the ecosystem knows
    what it is. Standard types are how your node talks to software you did not write --- which
    is the entire value proposition of Weeks 9 through 11.
  \end{inthefield}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Services and actions: the same idea with separators}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \column{0.47\textwidth}
    {\footnotesize\texttt{srv/ResetOdom.srv}}
    \begin{spec}
bool zero_heading
---
bool success
string message
    \end{spec}
    \vspace{0.2em}
    {\footnotesize Request above the line, response below.}

    \column{0.47\textwidth}
    {\footnotesize\texttt{action/DriveDistance.action}}
    \begin{spec}
float64 metres
---
bool reached
float64 travelled
---
float64 percent_done
    \end{spec}
    \vspace{0.2em}
    {\footnotesize Goal, result, feedback.}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{0.7em}
  \centering\small
  Three sections is what an action \emph{is}: something you ask for, something you eventually
  get, and something you hear about while you wait.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Interfaces live in their own package}
  \begin{shells}
ee414_ws/src/
  ee414_w03_interfaces/     <- ament_cmake. ONLY .msg/.srv/.action files.
  ee414_w03_nodes/          <- ament_python. Your Python nodes.
  \end{shells}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \footnotesize
  Two reasons, both of which matter before Week 12:

  \begin{enumerate}\footnotesize
    \item A Python package \textbf{cannot generate interfaces} --- generation is a CMake job,
          so definitions need an \texttt{ament\_cmake} package.
    \item Anyone who wants to \emph{talk} to your robot needs the definitions but not your
          code. They install one small package, not your whole project.
  \end{enumerate}

  \begin{underhood}
    \footnotesize
    This is why \texttt{geometry\_msgs} and \texttt{sensor\_msgs} are packages of their own.
    Same pattern, larger scale.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Reading a definition you did not write}
  \begin{shells}
$ ros2 interface show sensor_msgs/msg/LaserScan
std_msgs/Header header
float32 angle_min
float32 angle_max
float32 angle_increment
float32 range_min
float32 range_max
float32[] ranges
  \end{shells}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \small
  \textbf{Do this before writing a single line against an unfamiliar topic.} It is faster than
  searching, it is never out of date, and it is exactly the field list your code must use.

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \footnotesize
  \texttt{ros2 interface list} shows everything available; \texttt{ros2 topic type /scan} tells
  you which one a topic carries.
\end{frame}

\section{Services in detail}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The client is asynchronous. Always.}
  \begin{pycodes}
self.cli = self.create_client(ResetOdom, 'reset_odom')
req = ResetOdom.Request()
req.zero_heading = True

future = self.cli.call_async(req)
future.add_done_callback(self.on_reply)

def on_reply(self, future):
    self.get_logger().info(f'success: {future.result().success}')
  \end{pycodes}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \footnotesize
  \texttt{call\_async} returns immediately with a \textbf{future} --- a promise of a result
  later. You attach a callback and return control to \texttt{spin}, which is then free to
  deliver the reply.

  \vspace{0.2em}
  \textbf{This shape avoids failure 3.} Get used to it now; it returns for actions.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Calling a service without writing a client}
  \begin{shells}
$ ros2 service list
/reset_odom
$ ros2 service type /reset_odom
ee414_w03_interfaces/srv/ResetOdom
$ ros2 service call /reset_odom \
      ee414_w03_interfaces/srv/ResetOdom "{zero_heading: true}"
response: success=True, message='odometry zeroed'
  \end{shells}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \small
  You can exercise a server completely from the command line before its client exists ---
  which means you can test the two halves separately, and know which one is broken.

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \footnotesize
  Same idea as \texttt{ros2 topic pub} last week. \textbf{Build one half, test it from the
  terminal, then build the other.}
\end{frame}

\section{Parameters}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Never hard-code a number you will want to change}
  \begin{pycodes}
self.declare_parameter('max_speed', 0.22)
self.declare_parameter('frame_id', 'base_link')

speed = self.get_parameter('max_speed').value
  \end{pycodes}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \small
  A parameter is a named value the node reads at run time. Declared once, with a default,
  then read wherever it is needed.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{underhood}[Why this is not over-engineering]
    \footnotesize
    Every tuning constant in this course --- controller gains in Week 5, the obstacle threshold
    in Week 6, costmap sizes in Week 11 --- is a parameter. Tuning means changing a number and
    running again. If that requires an edit and a rebuild, you will try five values. If it is a
    parameter, you will try fifty.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Setting them from outside the code}
  \begin{shells}
$ ros2 run pkg node --ros-args -p max_speed:=0.15

$ ros2 param list /my_node
$ ros2 param get /my_node max_speed
$ ros2 param set /my_node max_speed 0.30      <- while it runs
  \end{shells}

  \small And in a launch file, which is where they belong for anything reproducible:

  \begin{pycodes}
Node(package='pkg', executable='node',
     parameters=[{'max_speed': 0.15}])
  \end{pycodes}

  \vspace{0.3em}
  \footnotesize
  \textbf{Setting a gain live, while the robot drives, is how tuning is actually done.} You will
  do exactly this in Week 5.
\end{frame}

\section{Actions}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}{What an action adds}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=0.55cm and 1.8cm, font=\scriptsize]
    \node[plan, minimum width=2.4cm] (c) {client};
    \node[act,  minimum width=2.4cm, right=of c] (s) {action server};

    \draw[dataflow] ([yshift=0.75cm]c.east) -- node[above,font=\tiny] {goal} ([yshift=0.75cm]s.west);
    \draw[dataflow] ([yshift=0.25cm]s.west) -- node[above,font=\tiny] {accepted / rejected} ([yshift=0.25cm]c.east);
    \draw[dataflow] ([yshift=-0.25cm]s.west) -- node[above,font=\tiny] {feedback, repeatedly} ([yshift=-0.25cm]c.east);
    \draw[dataflow] ([yshift=-0.75cm]s.west) -- node[below,font=\tiny] {result} ([yshift=-0.75cm]c.east);
    \draw[dataflow, draw=danger] ([yshift=-1.25cm]c.east) -- node[below,font=\tiny,text=danger] {cancel} ([yshift=-1.25cm]s.west);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \small
  Five exchanges instead of two. The server may \textbf{reject} a goal outright, reports
  \textbf{progress} while it works, and the client may \textbf{cancel} at any point.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \footnotesize
  Underneath, an action is built from topics and services. You never see that --- but it
  explains why an action has more moving parts than either.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Sending a goal from the command line}
  \begin{shells}
$ ros2 action list
/fibonacci
$ ros2 action send_goal /fibonacci \
      action_tutorials_interfaces/action/Fibonacci "{order: 8}" --feedback
Goal accepted with ID: 4f2c...
Feedback: partial_sequence: [0, 1, 1, 2]
Result: sequence: [0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13]
  \end{shells}

  \footnotesize
  \texttt{-{}-feedback} is the flag that shows what an action gives you that a service cannot:
  \textbf{you can watch it work}.

  \begin{inthefield}
    \footnotesize
    In Week 11 you send a goal to Nav2's \texttt{navigate\_to\_pose} action exactly this way.
    Feedback is the distance remaining; cancel is what a safety supervisor calls. Today's
    \texttt{fibonacci} is that interface with the robot removed.
  \end{inthefield}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The whole picture, one table}
  \small
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.7cm}p{2.4cm}p{2.4cm}p{3.6cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{Topic} & \textbf{Service} & \textbf{Action} \\
    \midrule
    Shape & stream & question & job \\[0.25em]
    Direction & one way & round trip & round trip + updates \\[0.25em]
    Waiting & nobody waits & caller waits & caller carries on \\[0.25em]
    Cancel & n/a & no & yes \\[0.25em]
    Progress & n/a & no & yes \\[0.25em]
    Typical & \texttt{/scan}, \texttt{/cmd\_vel} & reset, query & navigate, follow \\[0.25em]
    Cost & cheapest & cheap & most machinery \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \centering
  \textbf{Choose the cheapest shape that answers your question.} Most robot traffic is topics,
  and that is correct.
\end{frame}
