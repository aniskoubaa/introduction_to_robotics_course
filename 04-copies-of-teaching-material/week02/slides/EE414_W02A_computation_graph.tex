\section{Nodes and the graph}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}{Where we left off}
  \small
  The naive robot program failed for five structural reasons:

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{5.0cm}p{6.0cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{The problem} & \textbf{The answer we are about to build} \\
    \midrule
    One rate for everything      & Each node has its own timer \\
    One crash kills everything   & Nodes are separate OS processes \\
    One machine                  & Nodes discover each other over the network \\
    Nothing is reusable          & Nodes agree on message \emph{types}, not on each other \\
    You cannot see inside it     & Every message is inspectable from outside \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  Today is the vocabulary for all five. \textbf{Session B is where you type it.}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The node: the unit of a robot program}
  \keyline{A \textbf{node} is one process that does one job.}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \textbf{Good nodes}
    \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
      \item \texttt{lidar\_driver} --- talk to the LiDAR, publish scans
      \item \texttt{controller} --- turn a path into wheel velocities
      \item \texttt{battery\_monitor} --- watch the voltage, warn
    \end{itemize}

    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \textbf{A node that will hurt you}
    \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
      \item \texttt{robot} --- reads sensors, plans, controls, logs, and draws the user
            interface
    \end{itemize}
    \vspace{0.3em}
    {\footnotesize It works. Then the planner gets slow, and your control loop goes with it.}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{underhood}[The test]
    \footnotesize
    If you cannot say what a node does in one short sentence without using ``and'', it is
    two nodes.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

    \footnotesize
    Adding a fourth listener needs \textbf{no change to the publisher} --- which is how you

    driving it.
  \end{underhood}

\section{Four ways nodes talk}
% ===============================================================

\figslide{One: a topic --- broadcast, and nobody waits}{w02_pubsub}
  {The publisher does not know who is listening, or whether anyone is. That is the point.}
  {\pyocredit}

\figslide{Two: a service --- ask, and wait for the answer}{w02_service}
  {One client, one server, one question, one reply. The client blocks until it comes back.}
  {\pyocredit}

\figslide{Three: an action --- a long job you can watch and cancel}{w02_action}
  {``Mop the floor'' takes minutes. You get progress while it runs, and a result at the end.}
  {\pyocredit}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{And four: a parameter}
  \small
  A \textbf{parameter} is a named value a node reads at start-up, and that you can change
  from outside while it runs.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    \texttt{max\_speed} & \texttt{0.22} \quad metres per second \\
    \texttt{scan\_topic} & \texttt{/scan} \\
    \texttt{kp} & \texttt{1.5} \quad the gain in your controller \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.8em}
  \begin{inthefield}
    \footnotesize
    The test of whether something is a parameter: \textbf{would you want to change it without
    editing code?} A speed limit, yes. The number of wheels, no. Getting this wrong is why
    some robot code has forty numbers hard-coded in the middle of a control loop.
  \end{inthefield}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The four, side by side}
  \small
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.9cm}p{3.3cm}p{2.5cm}p{3.0cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Mechanism} & \textbf{Shape} & \textbf{Use it for} & \textbf{Example} \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Topic}
      & one-way stream, many-to-many, nobody waits
      & continuous data
      & \texttt{/scan}, \texttt{/odom}, \texttt{/cmd\_vel} \\[0.35em]
    \textbf{Service}
      & request $\rightarrow$ reply, caller waits
      & a quick question
      & ``reset the odometry'' \\[0.35em]
    \textbf{Action}
      & goal, feedback, result --- cancellable
      & long jobs
      & ``navigate to the door'' \\[0.35em]
    \textbf{Parameter}
      & a named value the node reads, and you can change
      & configuration
      & \texttt{max\_speed = 0.22} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  Topics carry almost all robot traffic --- and all of Weeks 4--6. Actions arrive properly in
  Week 11, when you ask Nav2 to drive somewhere and it takes forty seconds.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{How to choose --- four questions, in order}
  \small
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Does it arrive over and over, on its own?}\\
          {\footnotesize Sensor readings, velocity commands, poses. $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{topic}}
    \item \textbf{Do I need an answer before I can continue?}\\
          {\footnotesize And does it come back immediately? $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{service}}
    \item \textbf{Will it take seconds or minutes, and might I want to stop it?}\\
          {\footnotesize $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{action}}
    \item \textbf{Is it a setting, not an event?}\\
          {\footnotesize $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{parameter}}
  \end{enumerate}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{underhood}[The mistake this prevents]
    \footnotesize
    Using a service for something slow. The caller waits, its callbacks stop running, and the
    node stops responding to anything else. Every semester somebody builds a
    \texttt{drive\_to\_goal} service and cannot understand why their robot goes deaf.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

\section{Messages: the contract}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}[fragile]{A message is a typed contract}
  \small A topic is not a pipe for arbitrary bytes. It carries \textbf{one declared type},
  and both sides are checked.

  \vspace{0.3em}
  {\footnotesize\texttt{geometry\_msgs/msg/Twist}}
  \begin{spec}
Vector3  linear      # x, y, z   metres / second
Vector3  angular     # x, y, z   radians / second
  \end{spec}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  For a differential-drive robot only two of those six numbers mean anything:
  \texttt{linear.x} (forward) and \texttt{angular.z} (turn). The rest must be zero --- a
  wheeled robot cannot move sideways.

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{underhood}
    \footnotesize
    The light box means \textbf{not runnable}. This is an interface definition, compiled into
    Python and C++ classes at build time. That is why a typo in a field name fails when you
    build, not at 3 a.m.\ on the robot.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The types you will meet this semester}
  \small
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.9cm}p{1.7cm}p{4.6cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Topic} & \textbf{Carries} \\
    \midrule
    {\footnotesize\texttt{geometry\_msgs/Twist}}    & \texttt{/cmd\_vel} & Commanded $v$ and $\omega$ \\
    {\footnotesize\texttt{sensor\_msgs/LaserScan}}  & \texttt{/scan}     & One LiDAR sweep: ranges, angles, timestamps \\
    {\footnotesize\texttt{nav\_msgs/Odometry}}      & \texttt{/odom}     & Estimated pose and velocity, with covariance \\
    {\footnotesize\texttt{sensor\_msgs/Imu}}        & \texttt{/imu}      & Angular rate, linear acceleration \\
    {\footnotesize\texttt{nav\_msgs/OccupancyGrid}} & \texttt{/map}      & The map, as a grid of cells \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{inthefield}
    \footnotesize
    These are not this course's conventions --- they are the field's. A robot from a different
    vendor publishes \texttt{sensor\_msgs/LaserScan} on \texttt{/scan}. Learn the type once,
    and your obstacle-avoidance node works on hardware you have never seen.
  \end{inthefield}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Every message is stamped, and that is not a detail}
  \small
  Most sensor messages carry a \texttt{std\_msgs/Header}: a timestamp and the name of the
  coordinate frame the data is expressed in.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    \texttt{stamp} & when the measurement was \emph{taken} --- not when it arrived \\
    \texttt{frame\_id} & which frame it is measured in --- \texttt{base\_link}, \texttt{laser}, \texttt{map} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.8em}
  \begin{underhood}[Truth 2, in a struct]
    \footnotesize
    Last week: nothing is instantaneous. The stamp is how a robot copes --- it lets a node
    fuse a scan taken 100\,ms ago with odometry taken 20\,ms ago, \emph{correctly}. Week 7
    (TF2) and Week 9 (estimation) are built entirely on these two fields.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

\section{How nodes find each other}
% ===============================================================

\figslide{This process does not exist any more}{w02_roscore}
  {In ROS 1 nothing connected until \texttt{roscore} was running. \rosver{} deleted it.}
  {Screenshot: the instructor's ROS 1 course (2020), ROS Noetic. Shown as history, not as something you will run.}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{There is no central process}
  \small
  When a node starts, it announces itself on the network and listens for others doing the
  same. Publishers and subscribers on the same topic then connect \textbf{directly}.

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.6cm}p{4.1cm}p{4.3cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{ROS 1} & \textbf{ROS 2} \\
    \midrule
    Finding each other & A central \texttt{roscore} kept the directory & Distributed discovery --- no directory \\[0.3em]
    If that dies       & Nothing new can connect. The robot goes deaf. & There is nothing to die \\[0.3em]
    Start-up order     & Master first, always & Any order \\[0.3em]
    Delivery guarantees& One behaviour, take it or leave it & Chosen per topic (next section) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \footnotesize
  This is the main reason ROS 2 exists, and the main reason it appears in products where
  ROS 1 largely did not. It is a rewrite, not an upgrade.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{The consequence you met in Week 1}
  \small
  Discovery is \textbf{automatic} and reaches everyone on the network segment. In a shared
  lab, that is a problem:

  \begin{shells}
$ ros2 node list
/drive_forward          <- yours
/drive_forward_1        <- the person behind you
/turtlebot3_node        <- someone's robot, somewhere
  \end{shells}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    \texttt{ROS\_DOMAIN\_ID} & partitions discovery. Same ID $\Rightarrow$ you see each other. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \footnotesize
  \textbf{First diagnostic question of the entire semester:} a topic is obviously being
  published and your node sees nothing --- are you on the same domain ID?
\end{frame}

\section{Quality of Service}
% ===============================================================

\begin{frame}{The setting everyone skips, and then loses a day to}
  \small
  A publisher and a subscriber must agree on \textbf{how} messages are delivered, not just on
  the type. That agreement is the QoS profile.

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.4cm}p{4.0cm}p{4.4cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Setting} & \textbf{Options} & \textbf{What it decides} \\
    \midrule
    Reliability & \texttt{RELIABLE} / \texttt{BEST\_EFFORT}
      & Retry lost messages, or let them go \\[0.3em]
    Durability & \texttt{VOLATILE} / \texttt{TRANSIENT\_LOCAL}
      & Does a late subscriber get the last message, or nothing until the next one? \\[0.3em]
    History + depth & \texttt{KEEP\_LAST(n)} / \texttt{KEEP\_ALL}
      & How many to hold while the subscriber is busy \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  \footnotesize These are not performance knobs. They change whether your data arrives at all.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Choosing: it depends what the data is for}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \begin{block}{\small LiDAR scans --- \texttt{BEST\_EFFORT}}
      \footnotesize
      10 per second. If one is lost, another arrives in 100\,ms.\\[0.3em]
      Retrying a stale scan is worse than useless: it delays the fresh one behind it.
    \end{block}

    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \begin{block}{\small A map --- \texttt{TRANSIENT\_LOCAL}}
      \footnotesize
      Published once, rarely.\\[0.3em]
      A node that starts late must still receive it --- otherwise it waits forever for a
      message that already happened.
    \end{block}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{underhood}[The rule of thumb]
    \footnotesize
    \textbf{Streams} of sensor data: best-effort, shallow queue. \textbf{State} that is
    published once and must not be missed: reliable and transient-local. Commands
    (\texttt{/cmd\_vel}): reliable --- a dropped stop command is a crash.
  \end{underhood}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}[fragile]{The failure that looks like nothing at all}
  \footnotesize
  Publisher is \texttt{BEST\_EFFORT}. Subscriber demands \texttt{RELIABLE}. Both start
  cleanly, report no errors, and \textbf{nothing is ever delivered.}

  \begin{shells}
$ ros2 topic info /scan --verbose
Publisher count: 1
  Reliability: BEST_EFFORT
Subscription count: 1
  Reliability: RELIABLE      <- incompatible: no connection
  \end{shells}

  \footnotesize
  The subscriber asked for a guarantee the publisher does not offer, so the two are never
  matched. Not a bug --- the system refusing to pretend.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Silent topic: the order to check, every time}
  \small
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{ros2 node list} --- \textbf{is the publisher even alive?}
    \item \texttt{ros2 topic list} --- does the topic exist, spelled exactly that way?
    \item \texttt{ros2 topic info <topic> -{}-verbose} --- counts first, then QoS.
    \item \texttt{printenv ROS\_DOMAIN\_ID} --- \textbf{in both terminals.}
  \end{enumerate}

  \vspace{0.7em}
  \begin{center}\footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.8cm}p{6.0cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    Publisher 1, subscriber 0 & Different topic name, or different domain \\
    Both 1, no data & QoS mismatch --- read the two profiles \\
    Topic missing entirely & Publisher not running, or shell not sourced \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \centering\footnotesize
  \textbf{Learn this order now.} It is the same four commands in Week 11, on a robot with
  forty topics.
\end{frame}
